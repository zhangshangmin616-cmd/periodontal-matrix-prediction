# Keeping the panel forecast fixed while shrinking gene differences

This retrospective follow-up was specified after inspecting the original atlas scores in the destination studies, before computing the new transfer and shrinkage comparisons. The Hong Kong study contributes five paired 12-hour rank changes. The destinations are the two Wuhan time points and the separate single-library 12-hour stretch contrast. The Wuhan times share a baseline. These are three contrasts from two destination studies.

Use the original tissue atlas predictor in rank units. Write its forecast as m + d, where m is the mean across the 101 genes for a specimen or group and each gene has a deviation d. Compare m (uniform), m + d (full atlas), and m + a*d. Estimate one nonnegative a using only Hong Kong: minimise the sum of absolute gene errors across 101 targets and five donor-paired responses, keeping each donor's own predicted mean fixed. Use that slope without refitting in all three destinations. All ranks use the common 10,805 measurable genes. For comparison, compute the smallest error achievable if each destination were allowed to select its own nonnegative slope, explicitly labelling this a descriptive lower bound and not an external prediction. A weighted median gives the absolute-loss solution. To check whether high amplitude and gene assignment are distinguishable, report the loss at zero and at full amplitude as well as at the optimum, leaving the panel mean exactly fixed throughout. Repeat training with each source donor omitted in turn. Report all three destinations. Comparisons are descriptive and use the original datasets.
