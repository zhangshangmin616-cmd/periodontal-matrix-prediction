# Can measured donor responses improve cross-laboratory gene forecasts?

The 12-hour change observed in five paired Hong Kong donor cultures provides a measured comparator to the fixed day-3 atlas forecast. Three external contrasts were fixed before calculating the transfer scores. The destination responses and earlier atlas scores had already been studied. The shared ranking background contains 10,805 genes and all 101 predefined targets. RUNX2, outside the target panel, is the only gene from the earlier 10,806-gene universe without a unique measurement in the Hong Kong input. Recomputing the destination ranks on this shared background changes some reported values slightly relative to stage 17. All scores below use this common background.

| Destination | Zero gene error | Hong Kong gene error | Hong Kong common-shift gene error | Atlas gene error | Zero panel error | Hong Kong panel error | Atlas panel error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Wuhan 24 h | 2.800 | 3.245 | 2.802 | 4.637 | 0.291 | 0.098 | 0.239 |
| Wuhan 48 h | 2.564 | 3.062 | 2.617 | 4.229 | 0.449 | 0.642 | 0.501 |
| GSE106887 12 h | 6.370 | 6.350 | 6.340 | 8.330 | 1.420 | 1.228 | 1.730 |

Each number is an absolute error in percentile points. The Hong Kong forecast assigns each gene the mean of the corresponding five measured donor responses. The common-shift forecast assigns every target the same Hong Kong panel mean (−0.193 points), so both forecasts have identical panel-mean errors. At 24 h the Hong Kong gain of 0.193 points for the Wuhan panel coexisted with an increase of 0.446 points in individual-gene error. Its single-donor and single-library deletions retained both signs in all five and all six comparisons, respectively. At 48 h the observed mean changed sign while the forecast stayed the same, and both errors exceeded the zero-change errors in all such deletions. For the second 12-hour experiment, the small 0.020-point individual-gene gain exceeded zero in only two of five source-donor deletions (range −0.060 to +0.080 points). The common shift alone gave a gain of 0.030 points. This single-library-per-arm destination offers no independent estimate of variability between people.

At the individual-gene level the original Hong Kong shifts gave 54, 51 and 58 direction matches out of 101 at Wuhan 24 h, Wuhan 48 h and GSE106887 12 h, respectively. The corresponding atlas counts were 51, 56 and 49. Both sign counts and absolute errors condition on the same selected genes, which can have correlated responses. The common-shift comparator illustrates why a count of direction matches can depend on a predominance of one direction across a group of genes.

Run `python stage18/transfer/score.py` from the extracted cumulative archive with Python 3 and NumPy. The script verifies the archived NCBI run-count MD5s through the stage-17 readers and aggregates split runs of the same Wuhan library. It reconstructs the Hong Kong contrasts from `stage13/external/paired_counts.npz`, the donors’ matched gene counts and library totals. `stage13/external/sample_map.tsv` links source columns to donors and accession numbers. `stage13/external/source_manifest.json` records the public source checksums. `stage13/external/extract.py` documents construction of the archived compact matrix from the authors’ GEO counts and sample descriptions.

`scores.tsv` gives the 12 full comparisons. `genes.tsv` gives the observed and predicted changes for the same 101 genes for each of those comparisons. `deletions.tsv` gives all 108 scores after a single omission; the same destination contrast can appear in multiple rows because the four models were compared at every omission. `hong_kong_donor_changes.tsv` gives the source values for the five paired changes before any averaging. No biological sampling uncertainty is attached to the gene counts or the deletion ranges. The experimentally determined changes in the different protocols cannot distinguish time, tissue source, laboratory procedure and untreated baseline as causes of error.
