# Shortlisting large expression-rank changes

Run `python stage15/prioritisation/shortlists.py`, `python stage15/prioritisation/deletions.py` and `python stage15/prioritisation/check.py` in the extracted archive with NumPy. Original target-level observations, atlas predictions and control profiles are supplied with the preceding study modules. The output records every nominated or observed top gene and its fractional membership in the event of a tie. The reports at 5, 10 and 20 genes keep the same 101 candidate genes.

For ten nominations in Leuven at day 7, the atlas and the measured response shared SGCZ, with opposite predicted and observed directions. The predictor from Seoul recovered ADAM12 and VCAM1 with the correct signs. On each of the 19 exposed-specimen deletions the atlas recovered either zero or one signed match. The Seoul predictor recovered two on every deletion. Removing a Seoul training participant produced one to three matches. At twenty nominations both models recovered five signed matches. At five neither had a signed match. The selected shortlist length affects the result.

In Hong Kong, nominating ten genes from the four training donors recovered an average of 4.2 correctly signed large changes in the fifth donor. Assigning the predictions randomly within quintiles of untreated target expression gave 1.15 expected signed matches. The frozen in vivo tissue and fibroblast-like atlas each recovered 1.2 on average. The observed largest changes are noisy estimates from these particular samples. Retrieval is descriptive and counts of genes are not biological replicates.
