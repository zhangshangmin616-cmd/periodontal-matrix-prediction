# Six human progenitor-cell qPCR targets after seven days of clinical loading

Chen and colleagues at Peking University reported cultures derived from six loaded maxillary premolars and six unloaded mandibular premolars from the same three patients. Teeth in the exposed arm had received seven days of orthodontic force. The authors scraped the root surface, isolated cells, allowed them to adhere for three days and replated them. The publication provides no pressure or tension designation for each harvested human culture. Tooth arch and loading therefore vary together. The root-side-specific tissue sections in this paper are from rodents. The study is separate from the Nanjing atlas and provides a targeted ex vivo human expression comparison. [Methods and Figure 4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41368-023-00268-7) · [Original Supplementary Figure S3a](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC10788340.1/41368_2023_268_MOESM1_ESM.docx)

The six directly measured human progenitor targets are the four inflammatory cell-death genes in Figure S3a (NLRP3, CASP1, GSDMD and IL1B) and the two osteoclast-regulating genes in Figure 4d (TNFSF11/RANKL and TNFRSF11B/OPG). All six lie outside the fixed 101-gene target panel. The first four targets and RANKL had higher loaded-group means. OPG was essentially unchanged. Both the axis scale and plotted study condition were verified on the source figures. The methods list GAPDH in the human primer block and ACTB in the rat primer block. We computed log₂ ratios of published relative mRNA bar heights and divided each atlas target multiplier by the atlas GAPDH multiplier, after mixing sites. The RANKL/OPG ratio in Figure 4d reuses the two gene assays and Figure 4g measures co-cultured mononuclear cells. Neither entered the six-target endpoint. The paper reports n=3 human donors in its isolation procedure and n=3 biological samples in Figure S3a. The caption for the whole composite Figure 4 gives n=3–5 and the gene panels show different numbers of points. Public group bars do not establish the correspondence between points and donors.

At equal pressure and tension contributions, mean absolute gene error was 1.438 log₂ units for zero change, 1.109 for the fixed-RNA-share tissue prediction and 1.026 for the fibroblast-like prediction. Reassigning the same mean prediction to each of the six genes gave errors of 1.148 and 1.164, respectively. This shared-shift comparison was added after inspecting the six observed effects. The full tissue forecast gained 0.329 over zero and 0.040 over the shared shift. In this selected readout a substantial part of its gain could therefore be obtained from a common positive shift across markers. The tissue and fibroblast predictions describe cell groups in the atlas. Cells isolated in the external study were not assigned the atlas fibroblast gate.

Across all pressure fractions, including reasonable error in reading the plotted bars, a conservative lower bound on improvement for the fixed-share model was 0.051 log₂ units, and the corresponding upper bound was 0.576. These bounds describe figure reading and side weighting. Biological sampling uncertainty is unmeasured. The fibroblast-like result depended on the unknown site weights. With tension alone the error was 1.900, compared with 0.816 with pressure alone. Neither pure-side score is an observed human side contrast. At 50:50 the benefit of distinguishing genes persisted relative to the common model mean in this small selected group. The findings cannot identify accuracy across the 101 matrix genes, uncultured progenitors or donor-level changes. The original human figures are available under the authors' CC BY 4.0 terms. [Paper with PDF and supplemental material](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10788340/)

## Reproduction

The original unmodified research PDF and Supporting Information are by Liyuan Chen and colleagues, © The Author(s) 2024, *Force-induced Caspase-1-dependent pyroptosis regulates orthodontic tooth movement*, International Journal of Oral Science 16, 3 (2024). The package reproduces the original files in `stage15/day7/source/`. The bar coordinates, numerical results and scripts are a new analysis. Run `python stage15/day7/extract.py --cache stage15/day7/source` to read both original files offline. [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41368-023-00268-7) · [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) · [Original article PDF](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC10788340.1/PMC10788340.1.pdf) · [Original supplementary file](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC10788340.1/41368_2023_268_MOESM1_ESM.docx)

The compact input `coordinates.tsv` identifies the baseline and bar tops. Figure 4d preserves vector bar rectangles in the original PDF, so their heights were recovered directly from the page drawing commands. For Figure S3a the source is the embedded 2079 × 3780 pixel TIFF. Distinctly coloured horizontal lines identify the wide top edge, and black horizontal pixels identify the common zero. The grouped bar ratio cancels the y-axis scale. We varied each baseline and both top coordinates independently by ±2 native pixels in S3a and ±0.25 PDF point in Figure 4d. The method is retrospective and the source narrative was known when the checks were specified.

Run `python stage15/day7/score.py` and `python stage15/day7/check.py` from the extracted archive using Python and NumPy. The script recalculates target responses and errors from the coordinates, the earlier frozen tissue multipliers and atlas counts. The independent check reconstructs both models directly from integer counts and six baseline RNA weights. To regenerate the coordinates from the original source files run `python stage15/day7/extract.py --cache downloaded_sources` with NumPy, Pillow, PyMuPDF and network access, or pass a cache containing the source PDF and DOCX as listed in the script. Their SHA-256 hashes and the TIFF hash are verified before any readout. The hard-coded x coordinate regions mark the two labelled conditions in the published panels. No individual dots are digitized or paired.

## Additional candidates checked

- Militi et al. and Anastasi et al. had already provided day-7 protein measurements by root side. Prior analyses scored the identifiable FN1 measurement and documented uncertainty for collagen antibody chain specificity. Reanalysis of these figures would reuse existing evidence. [Militi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2019.e02572) · [Anastasi et al.](https://doi.org/10.3892/ijmm.21.5.545)
- Bumann et al. separated human PDL cells by compression and tension zones under in vivo loading. Their sampling interval was 14 days and the measurement was collagen synthesis. [Clinical study](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejo/19.1.29)
- The anatomically resolved matrisome proteomics study of loaded molars used mouse tissue at day 14. Its laser-microdissection design and primary readout do not match the human seven-day transcriptomic endpoint. [Matrisome study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9163570/)
- Public in vivo pressure-side and tension-side immunostaining and qPCR need to be screened by species and tissue in each figure. Chen's rodent panels and the prior day-7 human qPCR study by Zhu and colleagues cannot supply a second side-assigned human single-cell cohort for all 101 genes. [Chen et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41368-023-00268-7) · [Zhu et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13239690/)
