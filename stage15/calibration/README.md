# Can calibration in one laboratory rescue the atlas forecasts in another?

This module applies the previously fixed human periodontal atlas from day 3 to two public culture experiments. The 101 target genes never entered the calibration fits. All 217 measurable genes in the original extracellular matrix pathway were excluded, leaving 10,588 source genes for fitting. The full shared measurement background is 10,805 genes. Hong Kong provides five paired donors at 12 hours. Xi’an provides three controls, two libraries under 0.1 Hz and three under 0.5 Hz at 6 hours. Its two contrasts use the same controls and have no public donor assignment. The primary comparison scores the gene-wise group mean. Hong Kong donor-level errors therefore need not equal the error of the Hong Kong group average. [Atlas](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE327337) · [Hong Kong study](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejo/cjab043) · [Xi’an study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9259061/) · [Xi’an sequencing project](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/browser/view/PRJNA665587)

Training on the mean Hong Kong response gave no-intercept nonnegative least-squares scales of 0.00763 for the fixed-share tissue model and zero for fibroblast-like expression alone. Training on the equally weighted Xi’an conditions gave zero for both. Using absolute error for training gave zero for both in Hong Kong and scales of 0.00464 and 0.00206 in Xi’an, respectively. Across the three destination groups, two models and both training losses, the largest gain over zero was 0.000328 log₂(CPM + 1) units per gene. These estimates evaluate scaling alone, conditional on the fixed atlas, source gene set and laboratories.

| Destination | Gene error for zero | Fixed quarter, tissue | Fixed quarter, fibroblast-like | Best possible fibroblast-like error after fitting the destination itself |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Xi’an, 0.1 Hz | 0.26952 | 0.28383 | 0.32153 | 0.26949 |
| Xi’an, 0.5 Hz | 0.62655 | 0.62583 | 0.61638 | 0.61458 |
| Hong Kong | 0.12895 | 0.15649 | 0.19278 | 0.12873 |

The last column is an in-sample upper bound on the improvement attainable through a single nonnegative multiplier of that fibroblast-like response for those 101 observed values. Each destination has its own optimum. Fixed quarter-scale predictions were specified in the log-scale protocol and used in earlier work. At 0.5 Hz, the quarter-scale fibroblast-like forecast improved gene error from 0.62655 to 0.61638. Its error was also lower than that of a common forecast with the same mean (0.62062) and lower than the expected error after reassigning its forecasts among targets within baseline-expression deciles (0.65168). Each single deletion of one of the six contributing libraries preserved a gain over zero (0.00330 to 0.01255), and five preserved a gain over the matched-mean common shift. In the other two groups, quarter-scale fibroblast-like predictions increased error. At full magnitude both atlas models had larger error than their matched-mean uniform shifts in all three groups.

The exploratory rank-endpoint analysis was specified after the above results were available. Applying the quarter-size fibroblast-like multiplicative change to each untreated Xi’an profile gave a 0.5-Hz gene-rank error of 7.3003 versus 7.2795 for zero. The smaller log-scale error did not extend to these percentiles. At 0.5 Hz the quarter-size tissue forecast gave a rank error of 7.2403 and a log-scale error of 0.62583. These small gains depended on which library was retained. At 0.1 Hz and in Hong Kong both quarter-size atlas forecasts raised gene error on both scales. Hong Kong donor identity is known only for the Hong Kong series. The two Xi’an arms do not establish independent replications of a frequency effect.

## Offline reproduction

Run the three commands from the extracted evidence archive, using Python 3.10 or later and NumPy.

1. `python stage15/calibration/score.py` reconstructs the log-scale comparisons. It writes `parameters.tsv`, `scores.tsv`, `targets.tsv`, `assignment.tsv`, `oracle.tsv`, `donors.tsv` and `deletions.tsv`.
2. `python stage15/calibration/rank.py` reads the fitted coefficients and writes `ranks.tsv` and `rank_deletions.tsv`.
3. `python stage15/calibration/check.py` validates the shared genes, observed and frozen target profiles, parameter optimality, held-out scores, exact expected error of the reassignments, optima over all allowable destination-panel L1 breakpoints, donor scores and deletion sensitivities. It reconstructs ranks independently using average-tie ranks.

Inputs include the compact count matrices in `stage13/external/` and `stage14/xian/`, pathway labels in `stage5/reference/features.tsv`, and the atlas components in `stage8/axial_log2_multipliers.tsv.gz` and `stage10/reference_counts.npz`. Independent checks also use `stage12/culture/predictions.tsv` and `stage14/xian/target_changes.tsv`. Names in the output tables use HK for Hong Kong and XA for Xi’an. All losses labelled `gene_mae` average absolute errors over genes. `gain` subtracts this error from the zero-change error of the same genes and observations. `gain_over_uniform` compares with assigning every gene the mean forecast for the scored set. `constant_LS` instead assigns each target the average observed change of the training study's non-matrix genes. Positive gains favour the specified gene predictor. The independently fitted affine least-squares comparator includes a source-estimated intercept and a nonnegative slope. `protocol.md` and `rank_protocol.md` document when each comparison was chosen.
