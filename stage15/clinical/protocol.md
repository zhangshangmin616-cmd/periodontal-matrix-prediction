# Magnitude calibration of the frozen clinical forecasts

These rules were recorded before calculating this module. The target panel, the original forecast and the clinical outcomes have already been examined, including quarter and full forecasts, uniform-panel forecasts and a least-squares source fit in previous releases. This is a retrospective analysis of corrections to a fixed forecast. It adds no independent clinical dataset.

Use the archived 10,806-gene percentile-rank changes for Seoul (11 split-mouth participants at three weeks) and Leuven (one within-batch comparison at day 7 and one in a separate batch at day 28). Use the previously chosen atlas prediction for expression changes at fixed cell-group RNA shares, generated from day-7 side profiles with equal weighting of pressure and tension. Evaluate the original fixed 101 targets. The source calibration must exclude all 217 measurable matrix genes, leaving 10,589; no target RNA value at the destination may determine the source coefficient.

Use exactly the prior source–destination directions: Seoul to Leuven day 7, Seoul to Leuven day 28, and Leuven day 7 to Seoul. Fit a nonnegative scalar through the origin by minimizing mean absolute gene error on each source's non-matrix genes. Give each source participant equal weight before pooling their absolute errors (primary). Separately fit after averaging Seoul by gene (sensitivity). With predicted change p and observed y, every nonzero p contributes |p| weight at y/p, so a minimum is given by the weighted median of ratios restricted to [0, infinity). Use the smallest endpoint of any equally optimal interval; report the unconstrained signed solution as well. Evaluate scalar-times-atlas predictions on the original 101 targets. Compare zero, full, fixed one quarter, and each source-fitted scalar. For each scale compare against giving every gene that unit's common scaled predicted panel mean. The previous least-squares calibration is a reference only.

At each destination, report participant-first gene and panel errors (each participant's loss before averaging) and cohort-mean-first errors (average by gene, then score). Give Leuven one group contrast per time point. These are identical estimands for each Leuven time. In Seoul the contrasts come from measured split-mouth arms; no correspondence is inferred for Leuven between samples or times. Report target-specific same-direction and opposite-direction counts with a 1e-10 percentile-point detection tolerance; per-contrast descriptive counts and no gene- or participant-level p-values.

Compute two destination-informed lower bounds on mean absolute target-gene error, using the actual 101 outcomes. These answer how much a scalar could ever help and are not independent forecasts. For unrestricted nonnegative amplitude, fit b to b p; for preservation of the original predicted panel mean m, fit b to m + b(p − m). Apply the same scalar to all observations within an estimand; also report per-participant fits separately so the most optimistic option is identifiable. Compute the right directional derivative of gene MAE at b=0 to distinguish whether any initial positive movement improves the loss. Record a simple count and a |p|-weighted fraction of concordant directions. For each oracle also report best attenuation restricted to [0,1]. Interpret all scalar rank forecasts as comparisons at the scoring endpoint, and do not infer that a new complete transcriptome ordering has been generated. Explicitly calculate whether the target predictions obey 0–100 ranks, with separately identified clipped sensitivity in the event that a fitted forecast leaves the allowable range. Lower bounds concern the stipulated unbounded linear prediction families.

Source data and older analyses: [Seoul cohort](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm9020358), [Leuven cohort](https://doi.org/10.1111/jre.13352), [source atlas](https://doi.org/10.1177/00220345261491310).
