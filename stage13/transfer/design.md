# Cross-study transfer of observed clinical gene responses

Retrospective design recorded before computing these comparisons. The clinical outcomes and earlier atlas forecasts had already been inspected. Use the frozen 101-gene panel, common percentile-rank scale and existing baseline-calibrated atlas changes. Test whether earlier clinical measurements from an entirely different study improve gene forecasts compared with zero and with the atlas. Train on eleven three-week Seoul participants with equal weight and evaluate separately on Leuven day 7 and day 28. For the reciprocal study transfer, train on the single day-7 Leuven group contrast and evaluate on the eleven Seoul participants. Do not pool across times or train on the destination. Each Leuven endpoint is one between-group contrast. Reciprocal transfers reuse the same studies. This is an across-study transport benchmark, not new data or a temporally prospective surface-matched validation.

Assign each target gene the source clinical mean change for that gene or one quarter of that change, using the same value for every unit at the destination. Compare against zero, the uniform mean of the source panel, the unscaled destination atlas prediction and a fixed quarter of that atlas prediction. The source panel mean is one number averaged over the 101 genes and the source units. The quarter is a simple previously examined shrinkage baseline. Also calibrate the scale of the atlas in the training study by fitting a single least-squares slope through the origin across the 10,589 non-matrix genes and source units with equal weight. Constrain the slope to [0,1] and report the unconstrained estimate. Apply the frozen slope to the full gene-wise atlas prediction in each destination unit. No gene-specific fit, intercept, target coefficient, winning-model selection, or target-based tuning.

Score the individual-gene mean absolute error (MAE) and the absolute error in the mean signed panel change, averaging within units and then equally across the Seoul participants. The primary score uses the frozen change predictions as-is. As a sensitivity, clip all predicted changes at each target gene's marginal 0–100 percentile bounds using its destination untreated mean rank and retain exactly the same genes. A transfer predictor defined on rank changes is a score-space baseline, not a simulated full transcriptome. Report the source-target correlation of the observed gene changes as descriptive context and preserve all target forecasts. No p-values from treating genes, teeth or two Leuven batches as independent biological replications.
