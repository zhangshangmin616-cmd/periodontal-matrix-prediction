# Testing the anatomical interpretation of a pooled profile

The primary clinical endpoint in this study is the change in a gene's percentile rank relative to a shared 10,806-gene background. The 101 target genes and their order of analysis were fixed in the previous study materials. The original frozen forecasts assume equal mixing of pressure and tension profiles in linear abundance space. `score.py` calculates rank changes for each of five untreated Leuven baselines and compares their mean with the unseparated day-7 tissue contrast. The 19 loaded samples and five controls are different specimens in the same batch.

In `forecasts.tsv`, the first four numeric fields are model predictions. The final field is the observed unseparated clinical change. The table contains 303 rows, one for each of 101 genes under three assignments of the two root surfaces. `metrics.tsv` contains mean individual-gene error and absolute panel-mean error in percentile points. Swapping the labels or using identical copies of the mixed profile preserves every mixed rank and hence every pooled score.

`bounds.py` addresses a separate endpoint, absolute error in a root-side change on an ACTB-normalised log₂ scale. Its inputs are day-7 model effects and the two TGFB1 increases reported by Garlet et al. For the remaining genes, target effects can vary freely, with ACTB relative to itself fixed at zero. `bounds.tsv` gives the limits of possible panel gains over zero change. A positive upper limit for a side does not establish that the forecast improved. Some limits can be approached arbitrarily closely as a strictly positive observed response tends to zero.

Run `python stage16/sides/score.py`, `python stage16/sides/bounds.py` and `python stage16/sides/check.py` from the extracted archive. The independent check reads the clinical count files, reconstructs tied percentiles, recalculates ACTB-relative effects from the cell-group counts, and verifies all three bounds by evaluating the piecewise-linear gain. The clinical counts and donor metadata refer to the public [Leuven project](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/browser/view/PRJNA1136245). The source side libraries belong to [GSE327337](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE327337). The tissue PCR directions come from [Garlet et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0722.2007.00469.x).
